# The environment and sustainable development

**Sustainable development** meets the needs of people today without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs.

**Threats to Ghana's environment**

- **Galamsey** (illegal small-scale mining) pollutes rivers with mud and mercury and destroys farms and forests.
- **Deforestation** reduces rainfall and causes soil erosion.
- **Bush burning** kills plants and animals and leaves soil bare.
- **Plastic waste** blocks gutters and causes flooding.

**Solutions:** afforestation and reafforestation, enforcing mining laws, education, recycling, and involving communities. The **Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)** regulates activities that affect the environment.

## Worked example

**Explain one way galamsey threatens development.** It pollutes rivers that communities depend on for drinking water and fishing, which raises the cost of treating water and harms health and livelihoods.

## Try it

What is the difference between afforestation and reafforestation?

*(Answer: afforestation plants trees where there was no forest; reafforestation replants trees where a forest was cut down.)*

# Constitutional rule, democracy and the rule of law

- The **1992 Constitution** is Ghana's supreme law. The Fourth Republic began on **7 January 1993**.
- **Rule of law:** everyone, including leaders, is equal before the law and subject to it.
- **Separation of powers** and **checks and balances:** the executive, Parliament and the judiciary each limit the others. For example, Parliament approves the budget, and the courts can strike down unconstitutional laws.
- The **Supreme Court** has the final word on interpreting the Constitution.
- The **Council of State** advises the President.
- An **independent judiciary** decides cases without interference.

Threats to constitutional rule include coups d'état, corruption and disregard for court rulings.

## Worked example

**A minister ignores a court order. Which principle is under threat?** The **rule of law**, because no one is above the law.

## Try it

Which body has the final say on what the Constitution means?

*(Answer: the Supreme Court.)*

# Human rights and citizenship

**Human rights** belong to every person because they are human: the right to life, liberty, a fair trial, education and freedom of speech, among others. They are set out in the 1992 Constitution and in the United Nations' **Universal Declaration of Human Rights** (1948).

**Limits:** rights may be limited by law to protect the rights of others, public safety and public order. Freedom of speech does not allow false or hateful statements that harm others.

**Protection:** the courts, **CHRAJ** (Commission on Human Rights and Administrative Justice) and the **NCCE**, which educates citizens about their rights and duties.

**Citizenship:** a person is a Ghanaian citizen by birth if a parent or grandparent is a Ghanaian citizen. Citizens have duties too: obeying the law, paying taxes and voting.

## Worked example

**A suspect is held for months without being brought before a court. Which right is abused?** The right to a **fair hearing (fair trial)**.

## Try it

Name one institution in Ghana that investigates human rights abuses.

*(Answer: CHRAJ, the Commission on Human Rights and Administrative Justice.)*

# Population and national development

Ghana's 2021 Population and Housing Census counted about **30.8 million** people. The population is **youthful**: a large share of it is young.

- **Population growth** depends on births, deaths and migration.
- The **dependency ratio** compares dependants (the very young and the old) with people of working age.
- **Brain drain** is the loss of skilled people who go to work abroad.

**A large population helps development** when people are educated, healthy and employed. **It hinders development** when it grows faster than schools, health services, housing and jobs.

**Measures:** education and skills training, job creation, family planning, and developing rural areas to reduce rural-urban migration.

## Worked example

**How can a youthful population be an advantage?** If young people are educated and find jobs, they form a large, energetic **labour force** that can increase production.

## Try it

Give one effect of brain drain on Ghana.

*(Answer: for example, a shortage of doctors and nurses, or the loss of money the country spent training them.)*
