# Elements, compounds and mixtures

- An **element** is made of one kind of atom: oxygen, iron, gold.
- A **compound** is two or more elements **chemically joined** in fixed amounts: water, table salt.
- A **mixture** has substances that are **not** chemically joined, so they can be separated: air, muddy water.

**Separation methods**

| Method | Separates | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Filtration | an insoluble solid from a liquid | sand from water |
| Evaporation | a dissolved solid from a liquid | salt from sea water at Ada |
| Distillation | a liquid from a solution | pure water from salty water |
| Magnet | magnetic from non-magnetic solids | iron filings from sulphur |
| Winnowing | light chaff from heavier grain | rice |
| Chromatography | the colours in an ink | ink on filter paper |

## Worked example

**How would you get clean sand from a mixture of sand and salt?** Add water to dissolve the salt, **filter** to keep the sand, then dry the sand. **Evaporating** the filtered water gives back the salt.

## Try it

Is air an element, a compound or a mixture?

*(Answer: a mixture of gases, mainly nitrogen and oxygen, which are not chemically joined.)*

# Acids, bases and salts

- **Acids** taste sour and turn blue litmus **red**: lime juice, vinegar.
- **Bases** (alkalis when they dissolve in water) feel soapy and turn red litmus **blue**: soap, wood ash solution.
- The **pH scale**: below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral (pure water) and above 7 is alkaline.
- **Neutralisation:** acid + base → salt + water.

## Worked example

**Why do farmers add lime to acidic soil?** Lime is a base, so it **neutralises** the acid and makes the soil better for crops.

**Why does an antacid help with indigestion?** It is a mild base that neutralises excess stomach acid.

## Try it

A solution turns blue litmus red. Is it acidic or alkaline, and is its pH above or below 7?

*(Answer: acidic, with a pH below 7.)*

# Human body systems

**Digestive system:** mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (most digestion and absorption) → large intestine (absorbs water).

**Respiratory system:** air passes down the windpipe to the lungs. Oxygen enters the blood through tiny air sacs called **alveoli**, and carbon dioxide leaves.

**Circulatory system:** the **heart** pumps blood. **Arteries** carry blood away from the heart and **veins** carry it back.

| Part of blood | Job |
|---|---|
| Red blood cells | carry oxygen (using haemoglobin) |
| White blood cells | fight infection |
| Platelets | help blood to clot |

## Worked example

**Trace a piece of yam from the mouth until it is absorbed.** It is chewed in the mouth, swallowed down the oesophagus, churned in the stomach, then digested and **absorbed into the blood in the small intestine**.

## Try it

Which blood vessels carry blood back to the heart?

*(Answer: veins.)*

# Electricity and circuits

- **Conductors** let electricity flow (copper, iron). **Insulators** do not (rubber, plastic, dry wood).
- **Current** is measured in **amperes** with an **ammeter**, connected in series.
- **Voltage** is measured in **volts** with a **voltmeter**, connected in parallel.
- A **dry cell** changes chemical energy into electrical energy.

**Series circuit:** one path. If one bulb blows, they all go off.
**Parallel circuit:** separate paths. Each bulb can work on its own, which is why houses are wired this way.

**Safety:** a **fuse** melts and breaks the circuit if too much current flows. Never touch switches with wet hands.

## Worked example

**Two bulbs are in series and one blows. What happens to the other?** There is only one path, so the circuit is broken and **the other bulb goes off too**.

## Try it

Why is the wire inside a cable covered with plastic?

*(Answer: plastic is an insulator, so it stops electricity escaping and protects people from shocks.)*

# Health, nutrition and disease

A **balanced diet** contains carbohydrates (yam, rice), proteins (fish, beans, eggs), fats and oils, vitamins, minerals and water in the right amounts.

| Lack of | Causes |
|---|---|
| Protein | kwashiorkor |
| Vitamin C | scurvy |
| Iodine | goitre |

| Disease | Spread by | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Malaria | bite of the female Anopheles mosquito | treated nets, clearing stagnant water |
| Cholera | dirty water and food | handwashing, safe water, covering food |
| Bilharzia | water snails in infected water | avoiding bathing in infected water |

## Worked example

**A family keeps old tyres full of rainwater behind the house. Why is this a health risk?** Mosquitoes breed in stagnant water, which spreads **malaria**. Emptying the tyres removes the breeding places.

## Try it

Which food would help a child who lacks protein: cassava, fish or sugar?

*(Answer: fish.)*
