# Our physical environment

**Location:** Ghana is in West Africa. It borders **Côte d'Ivoire** (west), **Burkina Faso** (north) and **Togo** (east), with the **Gulf of Guinea** to the south. The Greenwich Meridian (0°) passes through **Tema**.

**Relief and drainage:** the highest point is **Mount Afadjato** in the Volta Region. The Black Volta, White Volta and Oti join the Volta, which flows into **Lake Volta**, created by the **Akosombo Dam**.

**Climate:** the south has two rainy seasons and the north one. The dry, dusty **harmattan** blows from the Sahara, roughly November to February.

**Vegetation:** rain forest and semi-deciduous forest in the south, coastal savanna along the coast, and **savanna grassland** across most of the north.

## Worked example

**Why do farmers in the north have one main growing season while farmers in the south have two?** The north has one rainy season; the south has a major and a minor rainy season.

## Try it

Name the dam that created Lake Volta.

*(Answer: the Akosombo Dam.)*

# The constitution and the arms of government

A **constitution** is the highest set of laws by which a country is governed. Ghana's present constitution was approved in **1992** and came into force on **7 January 1993**, starting the Fourth Republic.

| Arm | Job | Head |
|---|---|---|
| Executive | carries out laws and runs government | the President |
| Legislature (Parliament) | makes laws | the Speaker presides |
| Judiciary (courts) | interprets laws and settles disputes | the Chief Justice |

Sharing power among the three arms is called **separation of powers**. The President serves a four-year term, at most twice. Citizens aged **18** and above can vote in elections run by the **Electoral Commission**.

## Worked example

**A new law on plastic waste is passed. Which arm made it, and which arm makes sure people obey it?** **Parliament** made the law, and the **executive** carries it out (with agencies such as the police).

## Try it

Which arm of government settles disputes about what a law means?

*(Answer: the judiciary.)*

# Rights and responsibilities of citizens

**Rights** are what every person is entitled to: life, education, freedom of speech, a fair hearing. Children's rights are protected by the constitution and the **Children's Act, 1998 (Act 560)**.

**Responsibilities** are what citizens owe: obeying the law, paying taxes, voting, protecting public property, and taking part in communal labour.

Rights must be used responsibly so that they do not harm the rights of others.

Institutions that help: **CHRAJ** investigates human rights abuses, and the **NCCE** educates citizens on their rights and duties.

## Worked example

**A ten-year-old is kept home to sell in the market every school day. Which right is being denied?** The **right to education**. This is also child labour.

## Try it

Give two responsibilities of a citizen.

*(Answer: for example, paying taxes and obeying the laws. Voting and protecting public property are also correct.)*

# Population and development

- A **census** is an official count of everyone in the country. In Ghana it is carried out by the **Ghana Statistical Service**.
- **Population density** = number of people ÷ land area.
- **Birth rate** and **death rate** are counted per 1,000 people per year.
- **Rural-urban migration** is moving from villages to towns, usually for jobs and amenities.

**Effects of rapid rural-urban migration:** overcrowding, slums, traffic and pressure on water and electricity in the cities, and fewer young farmers in the villages.

A large population helps development when people are **educated, skilled and employed**.

## Worked example

**Why does the government need census figures?** To plan where to build **schools, hospitals and roads**, and how many are needed.

## Try it

Give one way to reduce rural-urban migration.

*(Answer: for example, provide jobs, electricity, water and good schools in rural areas.)*

# Colonial rule and Ghana's independence

Before independence, Ghana was the British colony of the **Gold Coast**.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1844 | The Bond of 1844 between some Fante chiefs and the British |
| 1900 | Yaa Asantewaa leads the war against the British |
| 1947 | The United Gold Coast Convention (UGCC) is formed |
| 1948 | Ex-servicemen are shot at the Christiansborg crossroads; riots follow and the **Big Six** are arrested |
| 1949 | Kwame Nkrumah forms the Convention People's Party (CPP) |
| 6 March 1957 | **Independence**, with Nkrumah as Prime Minister |
| 1 July 1960 | Ghana becomes a **republic**, with Nkrumah as President |

The Big Six were Kwame Nkrumah, J. B. Danquah, Edward Akufo-Addo, William Ofori Atta, Emmanuel Obetsebi-Lamptey and Ebenezer Ako Adjei.

## Worked example

**Why were the ex-servicemen marching in 1948?** They had fought for Britain in the **Second World War** and were demanding the benefits they had been promised.

## Try it

On what date did Ghana gain independence?

*(Answer: 6 March 1957.)*
