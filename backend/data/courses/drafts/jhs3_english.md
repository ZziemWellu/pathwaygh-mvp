# Reading comprehension

1. **Read the whole passage once** to understand what it is about.
2. **Read the questions**, then go back and find the part of the passage each one is about.
3. Answer **in your own words** unless you are asked to quote.
4. For "what does this word mean" questions, use the words around it (the **context**).

## Worked example

*"Esi woke up at five, swept the compound and fetched water before she left for school."*

**What did Esi do after sweeping the compound?** The order is: woke up, swept, fetched water, left. So the answer is: **She fetched water.**

*"The market women grumbled as the price of tomatoes rose."* **How did they feel?** To grumble is to complain, so they were **unhappy**.

## Try it

*"Although the road was rough, the nurses arrived on time to vaccinate the children."* What does the sentence tell us about the nurses?

*(Answer: they did their duty in spite of a difficulty. 'Although' introduces the difficulty, the rough road.)*

# Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms and words in context

- A **synonym** is a word nearest in meaning: *begin* and *commence*.
- An **antonym** is a word opposite in meaning: *ancient* and *modern*.

In the exam the word is in a sentence. The best answer must **fit the sentence**, not just mean something similar on its own.

## Worked example

*Water becomes **scarce** in the dry season.* Choose the nearest meaning: plentiful, hard to find, dirty, expensive.
"Scarce" means there is not enough, so the answer is **hard to find**.

*Our neighbour is a **generous** man.* Choose the opposite: kind, giving, rich, mean.
Generous means willing to give, so the opposite is **mean**.

## Try it

Give a word opposite in meaning to *victory* in: *The team celebrated its victory.*

*(Answer: defeat.)*

# Tenses and subject-verb agreement

**Concord** means the verb agrees with its subject: a singular subject takes a singular verb.

- *Each*, *every*, *one of*, *neither of* take a **singular** verb: *Each of the pupils **has** a book.*
- Two subjects joined by *and* are plural: *Kofi and I **go** to church.*
- With *either... or* / *neither... nor*, the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject.
- *News*, *Mathematics* and *furniture* are singular; *cattle* and *scissors* are plural.

**Time words** decide the tense: *yesterday* (past), *tomorrow* (future), *since 2023* (present perfect).

## Worked example

*One of the boys ___ lost his pen.* The subject is "one", so the answer is **has**.

*By the time we arrived, the film ___.* It started before we arrived: **had started**.

## Try it

Choose: *Neither Ama nor her sisters (is / are) at home.*

*(Answer: are, because the nearer subject, 'sisters', is plural.)*

# Letter writing

| | Informal letter | Formal letter |
|---|---|---|
| Who to | a friend or relative | a headmaster, official or editor |
| Addresses | yours only (top right) | yours (top right) and the recipient's (left) |
| Opening | Dear Kofi, | Dear Sir, / Dear Madam, |
| Heading | none | the subject of the letter |
| Closing | Your loving friend, | Yours faithfully, |

The **date** goes under your address. In a formal letter, keep the language polite and serious, with no slang or text-message short forms.

## Worked example

A good opening paragraph for a letter to the District Chief Executive about a broken bridge:

*"I write to draw your attention to the broken bridge over the river at our village, which has cut us off from the market."*

It states the purpose at once and politely.

## Try it

A letter begins *"Dear Sir"*. How should it end?

*(Answer: Yours faithfully, followed by your signature and full name.)*

# Figures of speech

| Figure | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simile | compares using *like* or *as* | as strong as an elephant |
| Metaphor | says one thing *is* another | The classroom was a zoo. |
| Personification | gives human qualities to things | The wind whispered. |
| Hyperbole | exaggerates on purpose | I've told you a million times. |
| Onomatopoeia | a word that sounds like its meaning | buzz, crash |
| Alliteration | repeats the first consonant sound | Peter Piper picked... |
| Euphemism | a gentle way to say something unpleasant | passed away |

## Worked example

*"The sun smiled down on the farmers."* The sun cannot really smile; smiling is a human action, so this is **personification**.

*"Ama is the light of her family."* Ama is called a light directly, with no *like* or *as*, so this is a **metaphor**.

## Try it

Name the figure of speech: *"She sings like a nightingale."*

*(Answer: simile, because it compares using 'like'.)*
