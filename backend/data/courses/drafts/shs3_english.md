# Vocabulary in context

In lexis questions the right answer is the one that fits **this sentence**, not just a word with a similar dictionary meaning.

1. Read the whole sentence and decide what kind of word is needed.
2. Use clue words (*although*, *unlike*, *because*) that signal contrast or reason.
3. Watch for look-alike words: *comprehensive* / *comprehensible*, *principal* / *principle*.

## Worked example

*The witness gave a ___ account of the accident, leaving out nothing.* Options: comprehensive, comprehensible, compressive, competitive.

"Leaving out nothing" points to complete, so the answer is **comprehensive**. *Comprehensible* means "easy to understand", which the clue does not suggest.

*Ama is a **diligent** student.* The opposite is **lazy**.

## Try it

Give a word nearest in meaning to *candid* in: *Thank you for your candid opinion.*

*(Answer: frank, or honest.)*

# Idioms and figurative expressions

An **idiom** means something different from its separate words. Learn them as whole phrases.

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| let the cat out of the bag | reveal a secret |
| burn the midnight oil | study or work late |
| a blessing in disguise | a bad thing that turns out well |
| bury the hatchet | make peace |
| add fuel to the fire | make things worse |
| a white elephant | a costly thing of little use |
| turn a deaf ear | refuse to listen |

## Worked example

*"The new stadium has become a white elephant."* It cost a lot but is **of little use**, perhaps because it is rarely used.

## Try it

What does it mean if two friends who quarrelled **bury the hatchet**?

*(Answer: they make peace and end the quarrel.)*

# Concord and tense

- **The number of** + singular verb: *The number of students **has** increased.*
- **A number of** + plural verb: *A number of students **are** absent.*
- Phrases like *together with*, *as well as* and *along with* do not change the subject: *The principal, together with the teachers, **is** coming.*
- *Either... or* / *neither... nor*: the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject.
- *Every... and...* takes a singular verb: *Every boy and girl **wears** a uniform.*
- Uncountable nouns (*furniture*, *information*, *news*) are singular.

**Conditionals:** *If it rains, we **will** stay.* / *Had I known, I **would have come**.*

## Worked example

*Either the teacher or the students ___ to blame.* The nearer subject is "students", which is plural: **are**.

## Try it

Choose: *The furniture in these rooms (is / are) new.*

*(Answer: is, because 'furniture' is uncountable.)*

# Reading comprehension

WASSCE comprehension questions often ask you to:

- **infer** what the writer suggests but does not say directly;
- explain the **writer's attitude** (critical, admiring, doubtful);
- give the meaning of a word or phrase **as used in the passage**;
- identify a figure of speech or the grammatical name and function of an expression.

When asked to replace a word, your word must fit the passage in the **same grammatical form** (a verb for a verb, the same tense).

## Worked example

*"Unlike his brother, who squandered his inheritance, Kweku invested his in a poultry farm."*

**What does *squandered* mean?** The contrast "unlike... invested" shows it means **wasted**. Note the past tense: *wasted*, not *waste*.

## Try it

*"Despite the assurances, residents remained sceptical."* How did the residents feel about the assurances?

*(Answer: they doubted them.)*

# Summary writing

1. Read the passage carefully and find the part each question is about.
2. Pick out only the **main points** the question asks for. Leave out examples, illustrations and repetition.
3. Write each point **in one sentence**, in your own words where you can.
4. Give exactly the number of points asked for.

## Worked example

*"Plastic waste blocks gutters, causes flooding, kills animals that swallow it and takes hundreds of years to break down."*

Question: *In one sentence, state the main idea.*

Answer: **Plastic waste harms the environment in many ways.**

## Try it

Summarise in one sentence: *"Regular exercise strengthens the heart, reduces stress and helps people sleep better."*

*(Answer: Regular exercise improves health.)*

# Letters, speeches and articles

| Type | Opening | Closing |
|---|---|---|
| Formal letter | two addresses, date, *Dear Sir/Madam*, heading | *Yours faithfully*, signature, full name |
| Semi-formal letter | your address, date, *Dear Mr Asante* | *Yours sincerely*, name |
| Informal letter | your address, date, *Dear Kofi* | *Your loving friend*, first name |
| Speech | a vocative: *The Headmaster, staff, fellow students...* | *Thank you for your attention.* |
| Article | a title, sometimes the writer's name | the writer's name (and class or address) |

Whatever the type, organise the body in **paragraphs**, each with one main idea, and keep the tone right for the audience.

## Worked example

**Opening of a speech for speech day:** *"The Headmaster, members of staff, invited guests, fellow students: I stand before you to speak on the topic..."*

## Try it

How should a letter to the Municipal Chief Executive that begins "Dear Sir" end?

*(Answer: Yours faithfully, followed by the signature and full name.)*
