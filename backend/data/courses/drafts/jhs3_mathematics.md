# Fractions, decimals and percentages

To **add fractions** with different denominators, first rewrite them with a common denominator, then add the numerators.

A **percentage** means "out of 100". To find a percentage of an amount, multiply the amount by the percentage and divide by 100.

Fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways of writing the same value: 3/5 = 0.6 = 60%.

## Worked example

**Add 2/3 + 1/4.** The lowest common denominator of 3 and 4 is 12.

- 2/3 = 8/12 and 1/4 = 3/12
- 8/12 + 3/12 = **11/12**

**Find 15% of GH₵ 80.** 80 x 15 ÷ 100 = **GH₵ 12**.

## Try it

1. Work out 1/2 + 1/3.
2. Find 20% of GH₵ 45.

*(Answers: 1. 5/6, because 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6. 2. GH₵ 9.)*

# Ratio, proportion and rates

A **ratio** compares quantities. To share an amount in a ratio:

1. add the parts of the ratio,
2. divide the amount by the total number of parts to find one part,
3. multiply one part by each number in the ratio.

In a **direct proportion**, when one quantity doubles, the other doubles too. Find the value for one item first (the unitary method).

A **rate** compares two different units, such as kilometres per hour.

## Worked example

**Share GH₵ 60 between Ama and Kofi in the ratio 2 : 3.**

- Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5
- One part = 60 ÷ 5 = 12
- Ama gets 2 x 12 = **GH₵ 24** and Kofi gets 3 x 12 = **GH₵ 36**. Check: 24 + 36 = 60.

**4 exercise books cost GH₵ 18. What do 6 cost?** One book costs 18 ÷ 4 = GH₵ 4.50, so 6 cost 6 x 4.50 = **GH₵ 27**.

## Try it

1. Share 45 oranges in the ratio 4 : 5.
2. A trotro travels 120 km in 2 hours. What is its average speed?

*(Answers: 1. 20 and 25. 2. 60 km per hour.)*

# Money: interest, profit, loss and discount

**Simple interest:** I = P x R x T ÷ 100, where P is the amount saved or borrowed, R the rate per year and T the time in years.

**Profit** = selling price - cost price. **Loss** = cost price - selling price.

**Percentage profit (or loss)** = profit (or loss) ÷ cost price x 100.

A **discount** is an amount taken off the marked price.

## Worked example

**Simple interest on GH₵ 2,000 at 5% per annum for 3 years:** 2,000 x 5 x 3 ÷ 100 = **GH₵ 300**.

**A trader buys a bag of rice for GH₵ 80 and sells it for GH₵ 100.** Profit = 20, so percentage profit = 20 ÷ 80 x 100 = **25%**.

**A dress marked GH₵ 250 has a 10% discount.** Discount = 25, so the customer pays **GH₵ 225**.

## Try it

A trader buys a radio for GH₵ 120 and sells it for GH₵ 90. Find the loss and the percentage loss.

*(Answer: the loss is GH₵ 30, and 30 ÷ 120 x 100 = 25% loss.)*

# Algebraic expressions

- **Substitution:** replace each letter with its value, then work it out.
- **Like terms** have the same letter (3a and -a are like terms; 3a and 5b are not). Only like terms can be added or subtracted.
- **Expanding** a bracket: multiply every term inside by the term outside.

## Worked example

1. If x = 3, then 4x + 5 = 4 x 3 + 5 = **17**.
2. Simplify 3a + 5b - a + 2b. Collect the a terms (3a - a = 2a) and the b terms (5b + 2b = 7b): **2a + 7b**.
3. Expand 3(2x - 4) = 3 x 2x - 3 x 4 = **6x - 12**.

## Try it

Expand and simplify 5(x + 2) - 3x.

*(Answer: 5x + 10 - 3x = 2x + 10.)*

# Linear equations

An equation is like a balance: whatever you do to one side, do the same to the other. Undo the operations one at a time until the letter is alone.

Always check your answer by putting it back into the equation.

## Worked example

**Solve 3x + 7 = 22.**

- Subtract 7 from both sides: 3x = 15
- Divide both sides by 3: **x = 5**
- Check: 3 x 5 + 7 = 22

**Solve 2(x - 3) = 10.** Divide both sides by 2: x - 3 = 5, so **x = 8**.

**Word problem:** Ama's mother is three times as old as Ama. Together their ages add up to 48. Let Ama's age be x, so x + 3x = 48, 4x = 48 and **x = 12**. Ama is 12 and her mother is 36.

## Try it

Solve 5x - 4 = 21.

*(Answer: 5x = 25, so x = 5.)*

# Angles, triangles and straight lines

Four facts solve most angle questions:

- Angles on a **straight line** add up to **180°**.
- Angles **around a point** add up to **360°**.
- The angles of a **triangle** add up to **180°**.
- **Vertically opposite** angles are equal.

## Worked example

**Two angles of a triangle are 65° and 48°. Find the third.**
180 - 65 - 48 = **67°**.

**Two angles on a straight line are 2x and 3x. Find x.**
2x + 3x = 180, so 5x = 180 and **x = 36°**. The angles are 72° and 108°.

## Try it

Three angles around a point are 120°, 95° and y. Find y.

*(Answer: y = 360 - 120 - 95 = 145°.)*

# Perimeter and area of plane shapes

- **Perimeter** is the distance all the way round a shape.
- **Area** is the space inside it, measured in square units.

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | length x width |
| Triangle | 1/2 x base x height |
| Trapezium | 1/2 x (sum of the parallel sides) x height |

## Worked example

**A rectangular garden is 12 m long and 7 m wide.**
Perimeter = 12 + 7 + 12 + 7 = **38 m**. Area = 12 x 7 = **84 square metres**.

**A triangle has base 10 cm and height 6 cm.** Area = 1/2 x 10 x 6 = **30 square cm**.

**A trapezium has parallel sides 8 cm and 12 cm and height 5 cm.** Area = 1/2 x (8 + 12) x 5 = **50 square cm**.

## Try it

A square classroom notice board has sides of 9 m. Find its area and perimeter.

*(Answer: area = 81 square metres, perimeter = 36 m.)*

# Circles: circumference and area

- The **radius** (r) goes from the centre to the edge. The **diameter** is twice the radius.
- **Circumference** (the distance round) = 2πr
- **Area** = πr²

In the BECE, π is often taken as 22/7, which works well when the radius is a multiple of 7.

## Worked example

**A circle has radius 7 cm.**

- Circumference = 2 x 22/7 x 7 = **44 cm**
- Area = 22/7 x 7 x 7 = **154 square cm**

**A round table has diameter 28 cm.** The radius is 14 cm, so the circumference = 2 x 22/7 x 14 = **88 cm**.

## Try it

Find the area of a circle of radius 14 cm. (Take π = 22/7.)

*(Answer: 22/7 x 14 x 14 = 616 square cm.)*

# Mean, median, mode and range

- **Mean:** add all the values and divide by how many there are.
- **Median:** put the values in order; the median is the middle one (or halfway between the two middle ones).
- **Mode:** the value that appears most often.
- **Range:** highest value - lowest value.

## Worked example

Kwame's marks in five tests were 4, 7, 7, 9 and 13.

- Mean = (4 + 7 + 7 + 9 + 13) ÷ 5 = 40 ÷ 5 = **8**
- Median: the middle value of 4, 7, 7, 9, 13 is **7**
- Mode = **7** (it appears twice)
- Range = 13 - 4 = **9**

## Try it

Find the mean, median, mode and range of 3, 8, 5, 8, 6.

*(Answer: mean = 30 ÷ 5 = 6; in order 3, 5, 6, 8, 8, so the median is 6; the mode is 8; the range is 8 - 3 = 5.)*
