# Atomic structure and the periodic table

| Particle | Charge | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Proton | positive | nucleus |
| Neutron | none | nucleus |
| Electron | negative | shells around the nucleus |

- **Atomic number** = number of protons.
- **Mass number** = protons + neutrons.
- **Isotopes** have the same atomic number but different numbers of neutrons.
- Electrons fill shells in order: up to **2**, then **8**, then **8** for the first 20 elements.
- The number of **outer electrons** gives the **group**; the number of **shells** gives the **period**.

## Worked example

**Sodium has atomic number 11 and mass number 23.**

- Protons = 11, electrons = 11, neutrons = 23 - 11 = **12**
- Electron arrangement: **2, 8, 1**, so sodium is in group 1 and period 3.

## Try it

Chlorine has atomic number 17. Write its electron arrangement and give the number of outer electrons.

*(Answer: 2, 8, 7, so it has 7 outer electrons.)*

# Acids, bases and salts

- Acids produce **hydrogen ions (H⁺)** in water. Alkalis are bases that dissolve in water and produce **hydroxide ions (OH⁻)**.
- **Indicators:** litmus (red in acid, blue in alkali), phenolphthalein (colourless in acid, pink in alkali), methyl orange (red in acid, yellow in alkali).

**Reactions of acids**

- acid + base → salt + water (neutralisation)
- acid + reactive metal → salt + hydrogen
- acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide

**Gas tests:** hydrogen burns with a squeaky pop; carbon dioxide turns lime water milky; oxygen relights a glowing splint.

## Worked example

**Zinc is added to dilute hydrochloric acid. Name the products and a test for the gas.** Zinc chloride (a salt) and **hydrogen**, which burns with a squeaky pop.

## Try it

A gas from the reaction of an acid with marble chips turns lime water milky. Name the gas.

*(Answer: carbon dioxide. Marble is calcium carbonate.)*

# Cells and their functions

| Part | Job | Plant | Animal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | controls the cell | yes | yes |
| Cell membrane | controls what enters and leaves | yes | yes |
| Cytoplasm | where reactions happen | yes | yes |
| Mitochondria | release energy (respiration) | yes | yes |
| Cell wall (cellulose) | strength and shape | yes | no |
| Chloroplasts | photosynthesis | yes | no |
| Large vacuole (cell sap) | keeps the cell firm | yes | no |

- **Diffusion:** particles spread from high to low concentration.
- **Osmosis:** water moves through a partially permeable membrane from a dilute to a more concentrated solution.

Cells form **tissues**, tissues form **organs** and organs form **systems**.

## Worked example

**Why do slices of pawpaw sprinkled with sugar become wet?** Water moves out of the pawpaw cells by **osmosis** into the concentrated sugar on the surface.

## Try it

Name two structures found in plant cells but not in animal cells.

*(Answer: any two of the cell wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole.)*

# Digestive, respiratory and circulatory systems

**Digestion**

| Where | Enzyme or juice | Acts on |
|---|---|---|
| Mouth | salivary amylase | starch |
| Stomach | pepsin (with acid) | protein |
| Small intestine | bile (from the liver) and pancreatic enzymes | fats are emulsified; all foods are digested |

Digested food is absorbed through the **villi** of the small intestine.

**Breathing in:** the diaphragm contracts and flattens and the ribs move up and out, so the chest gets bigger and air rushes in.

**Circulation is double:** the right side of the heart pumps blood to the lungs (pulmonary artery), and the left side pumps oxygenated blood to the body through the **aorta**. Valves stop blood flowing backwards.

## Worked example

**Why is the pulmonary artery unusual?** It is the only artery that carries **deoxygenated** blood, from the heart to the lungs.

## Try it

Which enzyme starts the digestion of protein, and where does it work?

*(Answer: pepsin, in the stomach.)*

# Electricity and electronics

- **Ohm's law:** V = IR (voltage = current x resistance) at constant temperature.
- Units: voltage in **volts**, current in **amperes**, resistance in **ohms**, power in **watts**.
- Resistors in **series** add: R = R₁ + R₂.
- Electrical energy is sold in **kilowatt-hours**.
- The **earth wire** and **fuse** protect users if a fault occurs.

**Electronic components**

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| Diode | lets current flow one way only |
| LDR | resistance falls in bright light |
| Transformer | changes an alternating voltage up (step-up) or down (step-down) |

## Worked example

**Why does a transformer not work with a dry cell?** A dry cell gives **direct current**. A transformer needs a changing (alternating) current to induce a voltage in its secondary coil.

## Try it

Two resistors of 4 ohms and 6 ohms are connected in series. What is the total resistance?

*(Answer: 4 + 6 = 10 ohms.)*

# Force, motion and work

**Newton's laws**

1. A body stays at rest or moves at a steady velocity unless an unbalanced force acts on it (**inertia**).
2. Force = mass x acceleration (F = ma).
3. For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

| Quantity | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Force | F = ma | newton (N) |
| Work | force x distance moved | joule (J) |
| Power | work ÷ time | watt (W) |
| Pressure | force ÷ area | pascal (Pa) |

**Weight** is the pull of gravity on a mass, so it is a force (in newtons). Mass is in kilograms.

## Worked example

**Why do passengers in a trotro jerk forward when it brakes suddenly?** Their bodies keep moving forward because of **inertia** (Newton's first law) while the vehicle slows down.

## Try it

A boy pushes a wall hard but it does not move. How much work does he do on the wall?

*(Answer: none, because work is done only when the force moves something.)*
